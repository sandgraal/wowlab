# Product review, 2026-11-12

For the owner. The owner asked the conductor for a complete review with
nothing held back: is wowlab feasible, can it be improved, or is it junk, and
would a real World of Warcraft player download it to interact with their game
and characters in one place?

**Nothing in this document is a decision.** The owner picks waves (ADR-0024);
the proposals here are written up as `docs/LAB_PLAN.md` §19 and become tickets
only after an owner pick. ADR changes named here would be `Proposed`.

Read for this review: `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`,
`docs/LAB_PLAN.md` §14 to §18, `docs/LAB_IDEAS.md`, the M16 section of
`docs/BACKLOG.md`, `docs/handoffs/M16-status.md`,
`docs/handoffs/outside-evaluation-2026-10.md` and its product seat in full,
plus live GitHub numbers and measurements of the repository.

### Where the M16 effort went

Of the M16 tickets closed, about ten change what a player can do: restore
into another flavor or install (M16-03), `snap export`/`import` (M16-04),
`addons check` and `sv list --orphans` (M16-34), every character folder in
`char list` (M16-35), `doctor` next steps (M16-37), discovery in more folders
(M16-112), the `wowlab ui` link that survives a reload (M16-113), characters
across flavors (M16-114), the confirm flow presented (M16-21) and the
Wellspring look (M16-52). The rest are hardening, wording, process and
follow-ups to reviews of earlier follow-ups. Examples: "Journal record ids
are ASCII digits only" (M16-70), "`snap export` archive members are 0600"
(M16-76), "The bidi scan reads what git commits for a symlink" (M16-104),
"The old 'no version in .build.info' words, made to match M16-118" (M16-121).
Each is defensible alone. Together they are most of a wave spent on an engine
that nobody outside the project has used.

## 2. Verdict

**wowlab is not junk.** Its engine is unusually good, and in places better
than anything players have. **As a product it is the wrong shape, it missed
its moment, and nobody knows it exists.** A typical WoW player would not
download it today, and one who did would likely give up within five minutes:
a terminal install, a file explorer as the home screen, and characters that
appear only after an addon install and a logout that nothing told them to do.

### What is strong: keep all of it

- **The safety core.** `guard` (one write gate, snapshot first, journal,
  atomic replace), undo that survived a `kill -9` mid-restore, and no
  network by default. WowUp's settings backup has no diff, no per-addon
  merge and no undo; CurseForge has no settings backup at all.
- **`luadata`.** Lossless, and it survived 80,000 seeded mutations. The best
  offline SavedVariables reader the panel's addon author had seen.
- **`docs/LAB_FILE_MAP.md` and `docs/LAB_FORMATS.md`.** The best public
  write-up of a modern client's `WTF/` folder.
- **`binds coach`.** No incumbent maps a player's own casts onto the cost of
  their keys.
- **The lab-addon.** It already records each character's gear, talents,
  customization, collections, currencies and professions at logout. That is
  the raw material for the product the owner describes, and today only the
  Character tab and three `char` commands read it.

### What is wrong, most severe first

1. **The product does not match the vision.** The owner wants players to
   "interact with their game and characters in a different way, bringing
   everything in one place". What exists is a toolkit for the files of an
   install. Players do not think in `config-cache.wtf`. The one feature that
   is literally "everything in one place", the alt-dashboard (§14.4), was
   deferred on 2026-10-14 as "a page over M12-09's data for one player's
   alts". That judgement is backwards for a public product: an offline view
   of every character, with history over time, is what a player would
   download.
2. **The process is consuming the project.** The constitution, 37 ADRs, the
   conductor, six agents, review rounds and the grader gate produce correct,
   well-reviewed hardening and rewording at high speed (§1, "Where the M16
   effort went"). The scarce resources are the owner's attention and the
   plan's usage, and both went to polishing an engine with no users. The
   panel's product seat raised this (F15). Nothing changed.
3. **It has never met a client that anyone is playing.** Beta fixtures only.
   The launch folder name, the launch interface number and whether the
   lab-addon loads on the launch client are unverified. Retail is
   unverified. No human has run it on Windows, which is most of the
   audience.
4. **The flagship promise is unproven.** Binds and macros may be synced by
   the server, and action bars and talents live on it. The two persistence
   tests (M13-07, `docs/handoffs/M14-binds-persist.md`) have never been run.
   If the client replaces a restored file at login, the first public
   reaction will be "undo doesn't work".
5. **The install filters out almost every player.** It needs uv, Python 3.12
   and a terminal. Players live in CurseForge and WowUp, which are one-click
   desktop apps. The owner's untracked `install-wowlab-app.sh` (a Mac
   launcher for `wowlab ui`) shows the same instinct, but it still needs
   git, uv and a clone.
6. **The voice is the maintainer's.** M16-15 to M16-17 improved the CLI's
   output, but the README's first screen is still dense and qualified
   ("Only two of them refuse when something changed after the plan…").
   M16-33, the rewrite, is open.
7. **The roadmap leans toward the owner's interests, not players' jobs.**
   The next pick on the table is "resume M15": a pure-Python CASC, M2, BLP
   and glTF pipeline that wow.export and Wowhead already provide. It would
   also retire the cleanest trust sentence on the page ("never reads
   `Data/`"). It is a fine personal-lab project and the wrong next public
   wave.

### Feasibility

Technically feasible, and most of the hard part is already built. The
platform's limits are real but do not get in the way of a companion app:
addon data arrives only at logout or `/reload`, there is no live channel out
of the game, and what the server owns cannot be restored. As shaped today,
the realistic ceiling is a few hundred technical users. Re-shaped as below,
it could hold a real niche of thousands, especially on Forever and Classic,
where Armory-style web tools are weak.

## 3. What a player would download

**"wowlab: your account, offline. Every character in one place, and you can
never lose your UI."** The product becomes `wowlab ui` as a desktop app, and
the CLI becomes the power-user layer.

1. **An account hub as the home tab.** Every recorded character across
   flavors: level, class, spec, item level and gear, gold, currencies,
   professions, collections, last played. Sortable and filterable.
2. **A timeline.** Snapshots plus dated lab-addon records give each
   character a history: item level and gold over time, "what changed since
   last week". Nothing out of game offers this. (`docs/LAB_IDEAS.md`
   digital-twin, slimmed down.)
3. **A UI time machine.** The existing snapshot, diff and undo, presented as
   one "back up before patch day or an addon update" button with a readable
   diff. The explorer moves to an "Advanced" tab.
4. **A patch-day check.** `addons check`, already built, as a card on the
   home tab: addons out of date or broken for this client, SavedVariables
   that failed to parse.
5. **The Character tab**, as it is: gear, keys, macros, the coach's
   findings.

Later, and only if users ask for it: bag-triage across alts, a weekly
checklist, `binds coach --apply` once the persistence tests pass, and the
simc bridge.

**Shelve:** the M15 chain (M15-06 to M15-13) and Wave 7, `char advise
--send`, crew-book, comp-builder and the db2lake SQL command.

## 4. Recommended order

### Step 1: stop the treadmill

- Dispatch no new hardening or wording ticket unless it comes from a user
  report or blocks the launch. M16-88 and M16-122 to M16-124 are small, ready
  and may finish. M16-87T and M16-87 stay deferred under ADR-0037.
- A bar for new tickets: it changes what a player sees, or a player reported
  it. This is a proposed rule for `docs/AGENT_WORKFLOW.md`, for the owner to
  accept.

### Step 2: v0.2 within about a week (mostly owner actions)

- **M16-05.** One owner session on the launch client: capture it, check
  that the lab-addon loads, log out once, and run the two persistence tests
  (M13-07 and `M14-binds-persist.md`). It is the most valuable hour left in
  the project.
- **The M16-45 family** (schema 5) only if that session shows it is needed.
  Otherwise tag v0.2 without it.
- **M16-33.** Rewrite the first screen around "never lose your UI" and "all
  your characters"; move "The full vision" to the wiki.
- **M16-32.** PyPI (`uvx wowlab ui`), GitHub Discussions, a 60-second GIF,
  and posts in r/classicwow, r/WowUI and r/wowaddons that also ask for one
  Windows tester.

### Step 3: the next wave is "companion", not M15

Written up as `docs/LAB_PLAN.md` §19 for the owner's pick: the account hub,
the timeline, the patch-day card, packaging (a double-click launcher on
macOS, a frozen binary on Windows; needs an ADR-0031 amendment), and the
Windows human run and retail capture recruited through the announcement.

### Step 4: a lighter process (owner decision)

- Keep test/implementer separation for `guard.py` and `luadata.py`, which is
  already the rule. Elsewhere, one reviewer rather than two or three, and no
  ADR for a change of wording.
- Cap the growth of `docs/LAB_PLAN.md`: fold dated amendments at each wave
  review. A wave review reports users, downloads and issues before ticket
  counts.
